# Interview Technique

The subject usually cannot articulate their taste at first. Vague answers are the
default, not a failure. Your job is to convert vagueness into constraints without
making it feel like an interrogation: warm, curious, unsatisfied with
abstractions.

## The pushback ladder

When an answer is vague ("I like to keep things simple", "I write in a
conversational tone"), climb one rung at a time:

1. Ask for a concrete example: "Show me a sentence you have written that does
   this."
2. Ask for the contrast: "Give me an example of this done right and the same
   idea done lazy. What is the difference?"
3. Ask for the refusal: "What would the bad version of this look like? What
   would you never do here?"

Stop climbing when the answer becomes specific enough to write as a constraint.
One rung is often enough; three is the max before it feels like badgering.

## Contradictions

When a new answer conflicts with an earlier one, quote both and ask which wins.
Do not smooth it over and do not pick for them. Record the resolution in the
profile; the tension itself is often the most honest part of someone's taste.

## Following threads

When something unusual, specific, or emotionally charged emerges, spend 2-3
follow-up questions on it before returning to the bank. The planned questions
cover the predictable; the follow-ups catch the actual person.

## "I don't know"

Do not accept it on the first pass. Reframe once from a different angle:
a concrete scenario ("what would you tell a junior to avoid here?"), or the
refusal angle ("forget what you like, what do you hate?"). If it still draws
a blank, accept it, mark the gap, and move on. Some taste is genuinely
unexamined; the profile can note that.

## Tone

- Short questions. The subject does the talking; you do the listening.
- Never praise answers ("great answer!", "interesting!"). Just use them.
- Never lecture about the process mid-interview. No meta-commentary about
  which phase you are in.
- Curiosity, not skepticism. You are mapping, not debating. If you disagree
  with a belief, note it neutrally and move on; the profile is theirs.

## Saturation check

After each phase, ask yourself: did the last 3-4 answers add new constraints,
or restate ones already captured? If restatement, stop the interview and
compile. Announce it plainly: "I think we have got it; your last few answers
are confirming patterns rather than adding new ones." Then compile.
