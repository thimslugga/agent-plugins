# Question Banks

Pick from these per phase; never read them out as a list. Follow the thread when
something interesting emerges, then return to the bank. Phase mapping:

- Phase 0 (warm-up): Beliefs and Voice
- Phase 1 (refusals, highest signal): Aesthetic Crimes, Hard Nos, Red Flags
- Phase 2 (mechanics): Writing Mechanics, Structural Preferences

After each phase, run the saturation check: if the last 3-4 answers only restate
known constraints, stop. Target 30-50 questions total, hard cap 60.

## Beliefs and contrarian takes

- What do you believe about your field that most of your peers would disagree with?
- What piece of conventional wisdom in your line of work do you think is wrong?
- What is a take you would defend even if the room turned against you?
- What did you used to believe that you have changed your mind about, and what
  changed it?
- What do people get wrong about what you do?

## Voice and personality

- When you are excited about an idea, how does that show up in your writing?
- When you are skeptical, how does the reader know?
- How do you handle disagreement: direct confrontation, steelmanning, dry humor?
- Do you use humor in your writing? What kind lands for you, and what kind makes
  you cringe?
- Describe your tone in three words, then give me a sentence that proves each one.

## Aesthetic crimes

- What sentence, phrase, or pattern in other people's writing makes you
  physically cringe? Quote it if you can.
- What is a piece of writing advice you think produces bad writing?
- What does lazy writing look like in your field? Give a concrete example.
- What formatting or stylistic habit instantly lowers your opinion of a piece?
- What is something everyone does that you think is a tell for not thinking?

## Hard nos

- What words or phrases would you never use, even if they were technically
  correct?
- What topics will you not write about, and why?
- What rhetorical moves are beneath you? (False balance, strawmanning,
  inspirational gloss, doom-mongering...)
- What is a line you will not cross to get attention?
- How do you open a piece? Now tell me three openings you would never use.

## Red flags

- What makes you immediately distrust a piece of content?
- What are the tells that the writer does not actually know what they are
  talking about?
- What does overconfident writing look like versus genuinely confident writing?
- What makes you close a tab within ten seconds?

## Writing mechanics

- How do you actually write: talk me through your real process, not the
  idealized one.
- What are your default sentence structures? Short declarative? Long and
  winding? Fragments for punch?
- How do you open pieces, and how do you close them? Show me a real example of
  each.
- What is your relationship with punctuation: em dashes, semicolons,
  parentheticals, exclamation marks?
- What words do you overuse? What words do you love? What words would you never
  touch?
- How long are your paragraphs, honestly? Check your last three pieces if you
  are not sure.

## Structural preferences

- How do you organize your ideas: narrative arc, argument then evidence, list of
  observations?
- When do you reach for lists, headers, or bullets, and when do you refuse them?
- How do you handle transitions between ideas?
- What is your default structure for a short piece versus a long one?
- What structural habit of yours would an editor have to pry out of your hands?
